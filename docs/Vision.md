# Vision for OpenPreEduLab

## Why OpenPreEduLab Exists

OpenPreEduLab was created in response to a structural problem in preschool education research: important studies often produce valuable but short-lived analytical artefacts. Data preparation procedures, indicators, model specifications, and interpretive logic are commonly developed for a single publication and are difficult for later researchers to inspect, reproduce, or extend.

This fragmentation is especially consequential for questions of preschool education resource allocation. Changes in population distribution, fiscal capacity, teacher supply, enrolment demand, and institutional provision interact over time and across places. These questions cannot be addressed adequately by isolated descriptive statistics alone; they require transparent data structures, defensible statistical models, and the capacity to examine alternative policy conditions.

OpenPreEduLab aims to turn such research work into cumulative infrastructure.

## A Research Infrastructure, Not a Chatbot

The project is not intended to automate scholarly judgement or to present unverified answers through conversational interaction. Its proposed role is to support a research process in which every substantive conclusion can be connected to its evidence, assumptions, and analytical procedure.

Large language models may eventually assist with tasks such as policy-document reading, variable extraction, interpretation of documented model outputs, and drafting of evidence-grounded research narratives. They are not the source of research questions, theoretical claims, causal inference, or policy decisions. Those remain the responsibility of researchers.

## The Research Problem

OpenPreEduLab is motivated by five recurring limitations:

1. Policy and literature evidence are often read and coded manually, which limits scale and makes procedures difficult to document.
2. Analytical workflows rely heavily on individual experience and are rarely packaged for reuse.
3. Statistical models and indicator systems often remain inside single studies rather than becoming reusable research assets.
4. Policy research frequently describes existing conditions without systematically analysing plausible future scenarios.
5. AI applications in education research often stop at text summarisation instead of being integrated with transparent statistical reasoning.

The project does not assume that technology alone resolves these limitations. Its premise is that open methods, explicit assumptions, and reusable research artefacts can improve the quality and cumulative value of research.

## Research Ambition

The long-term ambition is to develop an open framework connecting:

```text
Educational policy questions
  -> documented data and variables
  -> reproducible statistical analysis
  -> explicit policy scenarios
  -> evidence-grounded interpretation
  -> reusable research outputs
```

This framework is intended to support—not standardise away—the diversity of educational research designs. Different questions may require different theoretical perspectives, indicator definitions, spatial units, time periods, and methodological choices. OpenPreEduLab should make those choices visible and examinable.

## Initial Focus: Resource Allocation

The first phase centres on preschool education resource allocation. This focus is substantive as well as methodological. Allocation questions concern the relationship between educational provision and population needs, as well as the distribution of public finance, teaching staff, class capacity, and institutional resources.

The initial work will therefore seek to establish a carefully specified basis for evaluating resource allocation. Any future index or model must state:

- the unit of analysis and period of observation;
- the educational and statistical rationale for each variable;
- the treatment of scale, comparability, and missing data;
- the assumptions involved in aggregation or weighting; and
- the appropriate limits of interpretation.

## Long-Term Direction

OpenPreEduLab envisages four connected directions of work:

1. reusable statistical methods for allocation, equity, efficiency, and forecasting;
2. policy simulation for changes in subsidies, costs, demography, and provision;
3. AI-assisted evidence handling and interpretation that remains traceable to source materials and model results; and
4. an open community of researchers who can inspect, adapt, test, and extend these research assets.

These are aspirations rather than completed capabilities. The current task is to create the scholarly and technical foundations that make later implementation meaningful.

## Commitments

OpenPreEduLab is guided by openness, reproducibility, interpretability, and policy relevance. It seeks to ensure that research outputs can outlive individual papers, while respecting data governance, ethical constraints, and the irreducible role of human scholarly judgement.

# Inclusive Education Cognitive Interview and Item Revision Protocol

## 1. Purpose and status

This protocol defines a reproducible workflow for human cognitive interviews and versioned item-revision decisions after prospective expert content review. It is research infrastructure, not evidence that interviews have occurred or that any item is valid. The platform does not simulate participants, generate interview findings, or decide whether an item should be retained, revised, moved, split, or removed.

## 2. Research questions

Cognitive interviewing should examine how intended respondents understand an item, retrieve relevant institutional information, form a judgement, map that judgement to the response scale, and react to terminology, cultural context, accessibility, and safeguarding implications.

The process should ask whether an item preserves the module's central boundary: Child Participation describes meaningful opportunities and experiences in shared educational life; it is not a child-ability, normality, diagnosis, compliance, or worth judgement.

## 3. Governance and participants

Interview recruitment, eligibility, consent, compensation, accessibility, confidentiality, language support, withdrawal, retention, and safeguarding procedures require prior governance approval. Participants may include relevant preschool practitioners, policy or support professionals, researchers, and family or disability-community contributors. Participant roles should be recorded at a broad non-identifying level.

Do not enter names, contact details, child records, clinical information, case narratives, or unnecessary personal data. Use pseudonymous interview identifiers. Original qualitative evidence remains the responsibility of the approved research team and is not intentionally persisted by the Streamlit prototype.

## 4. Cognitive-interview record

The platform provides `inclusive_cognitive_interview_template.csv`. One interview may cover only the items actually discussed; complete 28-item coverage is not required. Partial coverage must remain visible and must not be interpreted as evidence about unreviewed items.

Each observation records:

- pseudonymous interview ID and broad participant role;
- interview round and instrument version;
- current item and dimension mapping;
- issue type;
- whether an issue was observed;
- a non-identifying evidence note when an issue was observed;
- an optional suggested revision;
- whether the observation is a minority or distinct view; and
- safeguarding and accessibility flags.

Supported issue categories are no issue, comprehension, retrieval, judgement, response mapping, terminology, cultural context, accessibility, and safeguarding.

## 5. Interview summaries

The software reports issue counts and coverage by instrument version, interview round, and participant role. Summary downloads exclude interview IDs, evidence notes, and proposed wording. Counts are descriptive records of submitted evidence, not psychometric scores, item-validity estimates, or automatic revision triggers.

A low issue count may reflect limited coverage or a homogeneous participant group. A high issue count may reflect productive probing rather than a defective item. Qualitative context and minority views must remain part of human review.

## 6. Versioned item-revision log

The platform provides `inclusive_item_revision_log_template.csv`. Research teams record each explicit version transition separately from interview evidence. Each decision includes source and target instrument versions, round, item, retain/revise/move/split/remove decision, rationale, minority view, safeguarding review, equity/accessibility review, responsible researcher ID, and date.

Revise and split decisions require proposed wording. Move decisions require a valid, different target dimension. Target-dimension fields are not permitted for other decisions. These checks improve auditability; they do not validate the substantive decision.

## 7. Decision principles

No issue count, I-CVI, CVR, or other threshold should automatically determine an item decision. The research team should triangulate:

- content-review ratings and qualitative comments;
- cognitive-interview evidence and coverage;
- minority or distinct perspectives;
- theory and construct boundaries;
- feasibility and likely data source;
- safeguarding, equity, cultural, and accessibility implications; and
- consequences for comparability with earlier instrument versions.

Consensus must not be manufactured by deleting dissent. Removed or superseded wording and the reasons for revision must remain reproducible.

## 8. Completion criteria

A cognitive-interview round is complete only when recruitment and governance records, interview coverage, non-identifying evidence, issue summaries, unresolved disagreements, and limitations are documented. An instrument revision is complete only when every changed item has an auditable version transition and required safeguarding and equity/accessibility review.

Completion does not establish reliability, construct validity, measurement invariance, external validity, or causal validity. Those require separately justified future studies.

# Inclusive Education Claim-Evidence Traceability Readiness Protocol

## 1. Purpose

This protocol records whether each planned research statement has a traceable
link to a specification, aggregate output reference, population and time scope,
uncertainty record, limitation, alternative explanation, and review boundary.

It is a prototype documentation audit. It reads no result values and does not
judge whether a statement is true, important, publishable, or causal.

## 2. Research problem

A report may contain careful methods yet still separate its strongest
statements from the outputs and limitations that should constrain them.
Traceability makes the proposed evidence chain inspectable without asking the
platform to generate or endorse the statement.

## 3. Record structure

Each row links a non-identifying claim record to:

- a reporting plan, execution record, and estimation specification;
- an instrument version and claim scope;
- an aggregate evidence-output reference and evidence type;
- population and time scope;
- uncertainty, limitation, and alternative-explanation records;
- Support Gap interpretation and source-provenance status;
- AI origin, human review, and public-disclosure status.

The claim label and researcher notes are validated but are not reproduced in
public audit outputs.

## 4. Claim scopes

The prototype distinguishes descriptive statements, association candidates,
mechanism hypotheses, research-question candidates, and records where causal
claims are explicitly withheld. These labels organise review; they do not
validate the content of a statement.

## 5. Evidence boundary

A reference to descriptive, uncertainty, sensitivity, qualitative, or
literature evidence does not prove that the evidence is accurate or sufficient.
A record marked as having no empirical evidence cannot support an empirical
finding. Literature and qualitative context must not be presented as a
statistical effect estimate.

## 6. Support Gap and causal language

Support Gap remains a descriptive or diagnostic indicator, not a causal
estimator. A traceability record cannot convert conceptual arrows or temporal
ordering into a policy effect. Causal language requires a separately governed
identification design, estimation process, sensitivity review, and independent
claim review.

## 7. AI and privacy

AI-assisted claim records remain drafts until human review is documented.
Human review does not prove truth or validity. Public outputs exclude free-text
claims, notes, institution identifiers, result values, and restricted evidence.

## 8. Limitations

A complete traceability record does not establish:

- source or result accuracy;
- complete reporting;
- measurement or model validity;
- practical importance;
- privacy adequacy;
- correctness of an AI-assisted statement;
- a policy effect or causal relationship.

## 9. Future validation

Future governed work requires a versioned claim inventory, reviewed aggregate
outputs, source provenance, uncertainty and limitation registers, privacy
review, and human sign-off. A release audit should compare the final report with
this inventory without exposing restricted or institution-level data.

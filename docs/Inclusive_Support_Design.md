# Inclusive Support Design Blueprint (v0.2 Discovery)

**Status:** Design-stage blueprint with a static boundary page; not an assessment, referral, or recommendation service.
**Scope:** Inclusive preschool education research and practice-support workflows.
**Data status:** This module does not collect, store, or analyse child, family, teacher, or institution-level case data.

## Purpose

Inclusive Support will help researchers and early-childhood education teams organise citable evidence, formulate inclusive-practice questions, and prepare human-reviewed team conversations. It makes the reasoning behind inclusive practice visible; it does not decide what an individual child needs.

The design begins from participation, access, belonging, communication, play, learning environment, and family partnership. It must not reduce a child to a label, score, or predicted outcome.

## Users and permitted uses

| User group | First-release use | Excluded use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Researchers | Organise literature and policy evidence; record research gaps. | Individual-level analysis without approved governance. |
| Teachers and support teams | Explore cited universal-design and participation-support resources; prepare team questions. | Diagnosis, placement, or disciplinary decisions. |
| Leaders and policy teams | Review non-identifying implementation questions and evidence gaps. | Ranking teachers, settings, or children. |
| Families and advocates | Read accessible explanations of boundaries and participation principles. | Submitting personal case information in the public prototype. |

The module may support questions such as “Which environmental or participation barriers should our team examine?” It must not answer “Does this child have a condition?”, “Which placement is appropriate?”, or “Which teacher is better?”

## First interaction model

1. **Frame a practice question** using broad, non-identifying areas: participation, communication, routines, play, sensory environment, or family partnership.
2. **Inspect evidence** through curated cards with citation, setting, claim boundary, limitations, and applicability notes.
3. **Plan a team conversation** using neutral prompts about barriers, adjustments, preferences, and family perspectives. No personal information is entered into the public app.
4. **Record human review** in a blank local reflection template recording sources consulted, uncertainty, participants, and a review date. It is not a case file or recommendation.

## Safeguards and hard boundaries

- No screening, diagnosis, risk scoring, profiling, classification, placement, eligibility, or automated recommendation.
- No collection, upload, transmission, retention, or publication of identifiable child, family, teacher, or case information.
- No teacher-quality score, ranking, or performance inference.
- No claim that a resource is suitable for an individual child. Decisions remain with families, authorised professionals, and relevant support teams.
- No external AI call. A future AI feature requires separately approved design, data-governance review, and visible human review.
- Accessibility is a release requirement: plain language, keyboard access, meaningful labels, sufficient contrast, and content not dependent on colour alone.

## Evidence-card standard

| Required field | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Citation and link | Traceable source, access date, and lawful reuse status. |
| Evidence type | Policy, guideline, systematic review, primary study, or practice resource. |
| Population and setting | State exactly; do not generalise beyond the source. |
| Claim boundary | State what the source supports and does not establish. |
| Limitations | Include relevant uncertainty or transferability limit. |
| Review record | Record curator, review date, and reason for inclusion. |

Evidence cards are information resources, not treatment protocols. Resources with unclear sources, inaccessible language, or unsupported efficacy claims remain unpublished.

## Governance and release gates

A designated human reviewer must check provenance, claim wording, accessibility, and absence of identifying case material before publication. Work involving research participants, institutional records, or child information requires documented and approved legal, institutional, ethical, security, retention, and consent arrangements; it is outside this discovery scope.

| Gate | Needed before advancing |
| --- | --- |
| Design review | Project lead approves scope, exclusions, users, and success criteria. |
| Safeguarding review | Appropriate expert reviews child-centred boundaries and escalation wording. |
| Accessibility review | Keyboard, contrast, plain-language, and screen-reader checks recorded. |
| Evidence review | Initial cards have complete provenance and independent content review. |
| Data-governance review | Required before any personal, institutional, or external-service data flow. |

## Delivery sequence and relationship to v1

1. Confirm this blueprint and nominate safeguarding/content reviewers.
2. Maintain the current static prototype of purpose, boundaries, workflow, and gates; complete and record its accessibility review.
3. Define the reusable evidence-card schema and curate a small independently reviewed, non-personal resource set.
4. Conduct accessibility and stakeholder feedback; record changes and unresolved concerns.
5. Decide whether any separately governed practice-resource workflow is justified; do not add individual data or automation by default.

This work may proceed in parallel with the v1 empirical data pathway. It must not use unreviewed raw/staging data and does not alter the existing `raw -> staging -> independent review -> processed` requirement.

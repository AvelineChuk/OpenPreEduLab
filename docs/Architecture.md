# Proposed Architecture

## Purpose and Scope

This document describes the proposed future architecture of OpenPreEduLab. It is a design document, not a statement of implemented functionality.

The architecture is intended to support a reproducible pathway from preschool education policy questions to documented evidence, statistical analysis, scenario-based policy simulation, and research interpretation. Its central design principle is separation of responsibilities: data, statistical computation, simulation assumptions, and language-based interpretation should remain distinguishable and traceable.

## Current Implementation

OpenPreEduLab is currently an implemented v0.1 research-software prototype with
an additional v0.2 Inclusive Education research module.

| Component | Current state |
| --- | --- |
| Project charter | Present |
| Foundational documentation | Present |
| Source code modules | Implemented and tested |
| Data ingestion and validation | Implemented for PRAI and inclusive research schemas |
| Statistical models | PRAI, equity, DEA, forecast, simulation, and inclusive dimension/gap prototypes implemented |
| Policy simulation | Conditional PRAI scenarios and exploratory inclusive-support scenarios implemented |
| LLM interpretation | Reviewable prompts plus optional visitor-controlled DeepSeek integration implemented |
| User interface or service | Streamlit Landing Page and Research Dashboard implemented and publicly deployed |

Implemented components remain research prototypes. Operational software status
does not establish empirical validity, causal identification, or policy effects.

## Design Principles

1. **Research question first.** Computational components should be derived from a substantive question and an explicit theoretical framework.
2. **Traceability by design.** Results should be linked to source data, transformations, model specifications, assumptions, and software versions.
3. **Separation of evidence and interpretation.** Statistical outputs are generated by documented computational methods; language models may interpret, but must not silently alter, those outputs.
4. **Reusable but configurable methods.** Common procedures may be implemented as reusable modules while retaining visible choices about variables, units, weights, and assumptions.
5. **Responsible data practice.** Data access, privacy, licensing, and aggregation requirements must be addressed before analysis.

## Proposed Components

### 1. Research Intelligence

**Research purpose:** organise policy and literature evidence that informs research design and variable definition.

**Potential functions:** literature mining, policy-document understanding, structured variable extraction, and research-gap support.

**Inputs:** bibliographic records, policy documents, researcher-defined questions, and controlled vocabularies.

**Outputs:** cited evidence records, candidate variables, definitions, and documented research-design notes.

This component should assist evidence organisation, not determine theoretical relevance or make unsupervised claims about policy meaning.

### 2. Data and Provenance Layer

**Research purpose:** make data inputs and transformations inspectable before they enter a model.

**Potential functions:** source registration, metadata management, schema validation, geographic and temporal harmonisation, missing-data documentation, and transformation logs.

**Inputs:** authorised administrative, statistical, survey, and research datasets.

**Outputs:** analysis-ready datasets accompanied by data dictionaries, provenance records, and validation reports.

This layer is proposed because policy-relevant results cannot be assessed without understanding the data-generating context and preprocessing decisions.

### 3. Statistical Modeling Engine

**Research purpose:** provide transparent, reusable analytical methods for preschool education research.

**Initial methodological direction:** preschool resource allocation evaluation using indicators related to fiscal investment, teacher resources, class size, institutional resources, and child population.

**Future methodological directions:**

- equity assessment using measures such as the Gini coefficient, Theil index, and coefficient of variation;
- efficiency assessment using approaches such as data envelopment analysis (DEA), subject to appropriate methodological justification; and
- forecasting of fiscal demand, demographic change, and teacher requirements.

Each method should expose its assumptions, parameter choices, diagnostic checks, and limits. Composite indices, in particular, should not hide normative choices about variable selection or weighting.

### 4. Policy Simulation Engine

**Research purpose:** examine how explicitly defined policy or contextual changes may affect system outcomes over time.

**Potential scenarios:** changes in free preschool education arrangements, fiscal subsidy levels, teacher costs, enrolment, and population distribution.

**Inputs:** validated baseline data, a documented statistical or structural model, and scenario assumptions specified by the researcher.

**Outputs:** comparative scenario results, uncertainty or sensitivity analyses where feasible, and records of assumptions.

Simulation results should be interpreted as conditional analyses, not predictions or causal guarantees unless the model design supports such claims.

### 5. LLM Interpretation Engine

**Research purpose:** help researchers translate documented evidence and model outputs into clear research findings and policy-relevant discussion.

**Potential functions:** result explanation, evidence-linked finding summaries, sensitivity-aware policy discussion, and research-writing support.

**Required safeguards:** the engine should receive structured, cited inputs; identify the evidence supporting an interpretation; preserve uncertainty and methodological limitations; and remain subject to human review.

The LLM layer should not calculate statistical estimates, invent data, substitute for validation, or produce conclusions unsupported by the recorded evidence.

## Cross-Cutting Research Records

To support reproducibility, the proposed architecture should preserve records for:

- research questions and theoretical assumptions;
- source data, licences, and metadata;
- variable definitions and processing decisions;
- model specifications, parameters, diagnostics, and software versions;
- scenario assumptions and sensitivity analyses;
- outputs and the evidence used to interpret them.

These records are not ancillary documentation. They are part of the research object.

## Development Sequence

The architecture suggests a staged development path:

1. establish documentation, terminology, data standards, and reproducibility conventions;
2. define and validate a first resource-allocation evaluation framework;
3. implement a minimal, testable statistical workflow around that framework;
4. add equity, efficiency, and forecasting methods only after their intended use and validation strategy are specified;
5. introduce scenario simulation with explicit assumptions and sensitivity analysis;
6. add LLM-assisted interpretation only after structured result records are available.

This sequence deliberately places methodological validity and auditability before automation or interface development.

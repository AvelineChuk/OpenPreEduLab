# Inclusive Education Results Reporting and Claim-Boundary Protocol

## 1. Purpose

This protocol records how a future inclusive-education analysis is intended to
be reported before numerical results are interpreted. It focuses on complete
reporting, uncertainty, privacy, Support Gap language, causal wording, and
human review of AI-assisted drafts.

It is a prototype reporting-plan audit. It receives no result values and does
not verify findings, significance, policy effects, or causality.

## 2. Research problem

Clear estimation and reproducibility records do not by themselves prevent
selective reporting, hidden deviations, compressed uncertainty, misleading
figures, disclosure of small groups, or overstatement of descriptive Support
Gaps. A separate reporting plan makes these boundaries visible for review.

## 3. Required record

Each row links one reporting plan to an execution record, estimation
specification, and instrument version. It records:

- primary, secondary, and exploratory analysis labels;
- outcome, null, and uncertain-result reporting scope;
- uncertainty and multiple-testing reporting;
- specification-deviation disclosure;
- subgroup and small-cell disclosure rules;
- institution-identifier and visualization-scale policies;
- Support Gap and causal-language boundaries;
- AI-assistance status and human verification plan;
- limitations, conflicts of interest, and review status.

Researcher notes are validated but excluded from public audit outputs.

## 4. Audit logic

The audit reports unresolved documentation prompts and a small set of boolean
documentation indicators. It does not assign a readiness score, pass/fail
label, publication decision, or preferred wording.

Support Gap must remain a descriptive or diagnostic indicator. A reporting plan
cannot convert it into a causal estimator. Causal language remains withheld
unless a separately governed design and independent review justify a different
scope.

## 5. AI boundary

AI may help organise or draft an interpretation only when its role and human
verification plan are documented. The workflow does not call an AI provider,
endorse generated text, or treat human review as empirical validation.

## 6. Privacy and disclosure

Public reporting must not expose child records, institution identifiers,
restricted notes, credentials, or small cells that violate the declared
suppression rule. Aggregate reporting does not automatically make data safe;
the relevant governance review remains necessary.

## 7. Limitations

A complete reporting plan does not demonstrate that:

- all findings were actually reported;
- uncertainty or multiplicity was handled correctly;
- figures are unbiased or accessible;
- privacy suppression is sufficient;
- Support Gap is valid;
- an AI-assisted interpretation is accurate;
- a policy effect or causal relationship exists.

## 8. Future validation

Future governed work requires a complete result inventory, reviewed tables and
figures, deviation logs, privacy checks, source-linked limitations, and a human
claim review. These materials must remain separate from clinical assessment,
child diagnosis, institution ranking, and unsupported causal conclusions.

# Inclusive Education Paired Longitudinal Bootstrap Protocol

## 1. Purpose

This protocol describes a prototype uncertainty audit for matched institutional
changes across adjacent administration rounds. It extends the longitudinal
panel-readiness and attrition audits without turning descriptive time order into
a growth, policy-effect, improvement, or causal model.

The audit asks how much the observed mean change in each inclusive-education
dimension and Support Gap varies when the matched institutions are resampled as
intact pairs.

## 2. Research boundary

The output is sampling-uncertainty evidence for the submitted matched panel. It
does not establish:

- longitudinal measurement invariance or score comparability;
- population representativeness;
- statistical significance or a validated decision threshold;
- improvement, deterioration, institutional quality, or effectiveness;
- policy effects, mechanisms, counterfactual outcomes, or causality; or
- an appropriate correction for attrition or missing rounds.

No child, teacher, family, or case-level data should be uploaded.

## 3. Required data

Records use the existing versioned institutional response structure:

- `pseudonymous_unit_id`;
- `instrument_version`;
- `administration_round`; and
- all 28 inclusive-education item responses.

The same stable pseudonymous institutional ID must identify a unit across
rounds. One instrument version is analysed at a time. At least two rounds and at
least five matched institutions in every adjacent observed-round pair are
required as prototype computation conditions. These are not claims of adequate
statistical power.

## 4. Scoring and matching

Inputs are validated and linearly transformed to the documented 0–100 scale.
The current equal-item scoring rules calculate five dimensions:

- Policy Support;
- Resource Support;
- Inclusive Practice;
- Child Participation; and
- Equity.

The Resource-to-Practice, Practice-to-Participation, and Overall Support
Conversion Gaps are then calculated. Rounds are ordered by their positive
integer labels. Only adjacent observed rounds are compared; absent rounds are
not created or imputed.

For institution \(i\), statistic \(s\), earlier round \(t\), and later adjacent
round \(t'\), the paired change is:

\[
d_{is}=y_{is,t'}-y_{is,t}.
\]

The point estimate is the matched-institution mean change:

\[
\bar d_s=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{is}.
\]

## 5. Paired Bootstrap method

For each adjacent-round comparison, matched institutions—not separate
round-specific rows—are sampled with replacement. Each selected institution
contributes its intact paired change vector for all eight statistics. This
preserves the within-institution pairing during resampling.

For each statistic the platform reports:

- point mean change, defined as later round minus earlier round;
- mean of the Bootstrap mean changes;
- estimated Bootstrap bias;
- Bootstrap standard error; and
- two-sided percentile interval at the researcher-selected level.

Researchers choose 100–10,000 resamples, an 80%–99% interval level, and a
non-negative random seed. The seed supports computational reproducibility. It
does not make the sample representative or the model valid.

## 6. Interpretation

An interval summarises the distribution generated by repeatedly resampling the
observed matched institutions under the specified procedure. Interval location
or whether it includes zero must not be converted automatically into:

- a significance decision;
- an improvement or deterioration label;
- evidence that a policy worked;
- a causal conclusion; or
- proof that the score has the same meaning across rounds.

Observed changes may reflect measurement change, administration differences,
sampling and attrition, institutional composition, contextual events, reporting
variation, or substantive change. Distinguishing these explanations requires
additional evidence.

## 7. Missingness and attrition

The paired audit uses only institutions observed in both rounds of each
comparison. It does not impute missing rounds, estimate response probabilities,
apply attrition weights, or infer MCAR, MAR, or MNAR mechanisms. Researchers
should interpret it alongside the longitudinal attrition and panel-composition
audit.

## 8. Privacy and export

Uploads are processed in the current Streamlit session and are not intentionally
persisted. Exports contain aggregate run settings, interval summaries, and
research-question candidates. They exclude pseudonymous IDs, matched-pair
trajectories, and Bootstrap draws.

## 9. Limitations

The ordinary paired nonparametric Bootstrap assumes the observed institutional
pairs are the relevant resampling units. It does not automatically address
clustered sampling, repeated dependence across more than two rounds, small-
sample bias, complex survey weights, informative attrition, changing intervals,
or multilevel structure. Percentile intervals may have limited performance in
small or irregular samples.

## 10. Future validation

Governed studies may require longitudinal measurement-invariance evidence,
cluster or block Bootstrap methods, multilevel models, survey-design-aware
variance estimation, missing-data sensitivity analysis, preregistration, and
independent replication. Method selection must follow the study design and
research question, not software availability.

Completion means uncertainty in the submitted matched mean changes was audited
reproducibly. It does not mean longitudinal change, improvement, policy effects,
or causal pathways have been validated.
